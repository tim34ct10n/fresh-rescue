The problem

Produce doesn’t spoil on a schedule. Markdowns usually do.

  • Visual checks miss early declineFresh produce deteriorates at different rates, even within one category, and staff can’t track every batch by eye.
  • Unnecessary markdownsWhole batches get discounted when only part of the stock needs it, which costs margin.
  • Missed sales windowsDeteriorating stock is often spotted too late to sell, so it ends up donated or thrown away.
How it works

From the produce bay to a live rescue offer, automatically

  1. 1

    Monitor each bay

    A sensor kit in each produce bay tracks temperature, humidity, ethylene (a ripening gas) and visual condition.

  2. 2

    Score every batch

    A quality model turns the readings into a 0–100 quality score and an estimate of remaining quality life.

  3. 3

    Mark down in stages

    As quality drops, the batch moves through 20%, 35% and 50% off. Low-quality stock is withdrawn for staff review.

  4. 4

    Shoppers rescue it

    Offers appear in a Fresh Rescue section on your store’s website, with the quality score and rescue price.

The markdown policy

Discount only what needs it, only when it needs it

The hardware

One compact sensor suite per produce bay

Off-the-shelf components keep the kit affordable for a single store.

Temperature and humidity

Sensirion SHT31-D

Tracks the storage microclimate of the bay.

Ethylene gas (C₂H₄)

SPEC DGS2-C2H4 · 0–40 ppm

A ripening indicator for the produce in the bay.

Camera

Pi Camera Module 3 · 12 MP

Captures images for visual colour and defect analysis.

Controller

Raspberry Pi 4

Runs the bay and sends readings over a wireless link.

Supplier-priced parts come to about $520 per monitored bay (NZD, excl. GST).

FAQ

Questions from store managers

Does Fresh Rescue replace our food-safety checks?

No. The quality score is a quality estimate, not a use-by date or a food-safety assessment. Your store’s normal food-safety controls always apply, and low-quality batches are withdrawn for staff review.

What do my staff need to do?

Very little day to day. Markdowns update automatically. Staff review batches that are withdrawn and decide what happens to them, for example donating them to a food rescue charity.

Do shoppers need to download an app?

No. Rescue offers appear in a Fresh Rescue section on your store’s website, so shoppers can browse them on any phone or computer.

Which produce does it cover?

The pilot starts with two bays. Our prototype uses Royal Gala apples and Cavendish bananas. More categories are added after the pilot.

Is the system proven?

Not yet in a store. Our working prototype uses simulated sensor data. The pilot is where we calibrate the model with real produce and measure the effect on waste and sales.
